## Featured Projects

### 1. St. Louis Cardinals Tender/Non-Tender Decision Analysis (2026)

**Independent Baseball Operations Research**

An independent evaluation of the Cardinals' 2026 end-of-season roster and tender decisions using publicly available performance, contract, service-time, and projection data.

I developed player evaluations that consider expected performance, arbitration costs, organizational depth, roster flexibility, and potential internal alternatives. The final report presents individual tender/non-tender recommendations and the reasoning behind them, while acknowledging the limitations of public information.

The project includes representative Jupyter notebooks demonstrating the analytical process used to evaluate individual players.

### 2. Starting Pitcher Removal Decision Model (2025)

**Graduate Course Final Project — Deep Reinforcement Learning | Washington University in St. Louis**

Developed a Deep Q-Network (DQN) using 2025 MLB Statcast data to investigate starting pitcher removal decisions as a sequential decision-making problem.

The model evaluates whether to retain or remove a starting pitcher at the end of an inning using pitch count, times through the order, inning, score differential, and game context. I explored different reward structures and compared the resulting policies with historical managerial decisions.

The project examines the potential for reinforcement learning in baseball strategy while identifying important limitations, including the absence of counterfactual outcomes and complete bullpen constraints.

### 3. Winning and Player Compensation During the Moneyball and Shift Eras (2023)

Co-authored an economics research paper examining how the statistical factors associated with team success and player compensation changed across different eras of Major League Baseball.

I conducted the statistical analysis and collaborated on writing the paper. The research uses regression models to examine relationships between performance metrics, team winning percentage, and player salaries, with implications for MLB's 2023 rule changes.

### 4. The Effect of Different Training Modalities on Force Plate CMJ Metrics (2024)

Co-authored a study examining the effects of different strength-training modalities on countermovement jump metrics among 39 collegiate baseball players.

I was a primary contributor to the data collection alongside another researcher and collaborated with my co-authors on writing the paper. Our faculty advisor conducted the statistical analysis.

The research explores how testing timing and training modality affect force plate measurements used to monitor athlete performance and development.
